Sound familiar?

If depression has outlasted every prescription, you're not failing treatment. The treatment failed you.

Roughly one in three people with major depressive disorder never get adequate relief from antidepressants, no matter how many they try. Here's what that looks like from the inside.

"Let's try one more."

Two medications. Then three. Then four. Every switch costs you six to eight weeks of waiting, a fresh set of side effects, and a little more of the belief that anything will work.

The side effects cost more than the relief

Weight you didn't ask for. A libido that vanished. That flat, cotton-wool feeling where you're not sad any more, but you're not anything else either.

Functioning on the outside. Drowning underneath.

You show up to work. You answer the texts. Nobody around you knows how much of the day is spent just getting through the day.

"Maybe this is just how I am."

The most dangerous idea depression ever sells you: that years of failed prescriptions are evidence about you, rather than evidence about the method.

It isn't how you are. Depression is driven by insufficient activity in the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, and antidepressants work through your bloodstream hoping to reach it. TMS stimulates that exact region directly.

How TMS works

A different mechanism, not another pill

Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation uses precisely targeted magnetic pulses to stimulate underactive mood circuits, prompting the brain to form new, healthier neural pathways.

  1. 1

    Magnetic field generation

    A powerful magnetic coil creates targeted pulses, the same type of field used in an MRI.

  2. 2

    Neural stimulation

    Those pulses pass painlessly through the skull and stimulate underactive brain regions.

  3. 3

    Neuroplasticity changes

    The brain responds by forming new, healthier neural pathways in the mood circuit.

  4. 4

    Lasting improvement

    Those changes hold, which is why remission commonly lasts 12 months or more.

TMS is built for you if…

  • You've been diagnosed with major depressive disorder
  • You've tried two or more antidepressants without adequate relief
  • Side effects have made medication hard to stay on
  • You're an adult living in or near the Coachella Valley
  • You can attend weekday sessions for six to eight weeks

TMS isn't the right fit if…

  • You have non-removable metal or an implanted device in or near your head, such as cochlear implants, aneurysm clips or stimulators
  • You have a history of seizures or epilepsy, which needs individual review first
  • You're looking for a single-visit or overnight fix
  • You can't commit to consistent weekday appointments right now

Common questions

Straight answers before you call

Is TMS therapy painful?

No. Most patients describe a tapping or knocking sensation on the scalp that feels strange for the first few sessions and then becomes unremarkable. There's no anesthesia and no sedation, so you're awake and able to talk throughout. Some people get a mild headache early on that usually settles within the first week.

How long does a typical TMS session last?

About 20 minutes. A standard course is 36 sessions, Monday to Friday over six to eight weeks. There's no downtime, so you can drive yourself home and go straight back to work.

Will my insurance cover TMS?

Most major plans cover TMS for treatment-resistant depression, and Karma TMS accepts Medicare, IEHP, Tricare, Blue Cross Blue Shield, Aetna and Cigna. Medicare Part B covers FDA-cleared TMS for major depressive disorder. Requirements vary by plan, typically two or more antidepressant trials that haven't worked. We verify your benefits free and handle every prior authorization for you.

Is Dr. Sunder a licensed, board-certified psychiatrist?

Yes. Dr. Keerthy Sunder is board-certified in Psychiatry and Addiction Medicine, completed residency training at the University of Pittsburgh, serves on the board of the Society for Brain Mapping & Therapeutics, and is faculty at the UC Riverside School of Medicine. He has more than 30 years of experience and has treated over 500 patients.

How quickly can I schedule an appointment?

We reply to every request within one business day. Office hours are Monday to Friday, 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM, and we have three Coachella Valley locations in Palm Springs, Rancho Mirage and Twentynine Palms, so a nearby slot is usually available quickly. Take the eligibility check and a coordinator will be in touch.

How is TMS different from ECT (shock therapy)?

They aren't comparable procedures. ECT requires general anesthesia, induces a seizure, and carries a risk of memory loss. TMS uses focused magnetic pulses on a specific brain region while you sit awake in a chair, with no anesthesia, no induced seizure, no memory effects, and you drive yourself home afterwards.

Do I have to stop my antidepressants?

No. Most patients stay on their current medication throughout TMS. If reducing anything makes sense later, it's done gradually with your prescribing clinician, never abruptly and never on your own.

How long until I feel something, and does it last?

Many patients notice the first change between weeks two and four, and improvement often continues after the course ends. Clinical results show around 50% of patients reach complete remission, with benefits lasting 12 months or more. Some patients later need maintenance sessions, and re-treatment generally works again for people who responded the first time.

What are the actual risks?

The common side effects are scalp discomfort and headache during the first week. The serious risk is seizure, which is rare. Screening exists to identify anyone for whom that risk is elevated, including people with a seizure history or non-removable metal near the head. Dr. Sunder reviews this with you in detail before treatment begins.
